Project Context & Background

Why This Project Existed

Kyobee is a guest waitlist management application built for hotels, bars, and restaurants. It allows venue operators to manage guest queues digitally, eliminating paper waitlists and reducing walk-away rates. The platform operates on a SaaS subscription model with monthly and annual billing tiers targeted at venue managers and hospitality business owners.

The Problem Statement

Friction & Roadblocks Faced by Users

The existing sign-up and subscription flow had a 67% abandonment rate at the plan selection step. Venue managers — typically non-technical business owners working in fast-paced environments — were overwhelmed by a cluttered feature comparison table, hidden renewal terms buried in fine print, and a 4-step checkout form that required too much cognitive effort during busy service hours. The hero section on the landing page also failed to communicate Kyobee's core value proposition within the first 3 seconds.

Research & Discovery

How We Listened to the User

▸Funnel Drop-Off Analysis: Used Hotjar session recordings and Google Analytics funnel reports to pinpoint exact abandonment points. The plan selector page had the highest exit rate (67%), followed by the payment info form (34%).
▸User Feedback Synthesis: Analyzed 48 support tickets and 12 Intercom chat transcripts where users explicitly mentioned confusion about pricing, billing cycles, and hidden fees.
▸Competitive Audit: Benchmarked plan selector UX patterns from 8 successful SaaS products (Slack, Notion, Canva, Monday.com). Key pattern: the best converters used progressive disclosure — showing only essential plan differences upfront with expandable detail rows.
▸Stakeholder Interviews: Conducted 3 interviews with the Kyobee product team to understand business constraints (minimum viable checkout fields, compliance requirements for payment processing).
Step-by-Step Architecture

The Design & Iteration Process

Heuristic Evaluation

Week 1

Audited the existing plan selector against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics. Found 14 violations including: lack of error prevention in payment forms, no visible system status during processing, and inconsistent terminology between marketing copy and UI labels.

Information Architecture

Week 1–2

Restructured the plan comparison to highlight only 4 key differentiators per tier (guests/month, SMS notifications, analytics dashboard, priority support). Moved detailed feature lists to an expandable 'Compare all features' accordion.

Wireframing & Prototyping

Week 2–3

Created 3 wireframe variants for the plan selector: horizontal card layout, vertical tiered list, and a toggle-based comparison. User-tested all 3 with 5 venue managers via unmoderated remote testing. The card layout with monthly/annual toggle won decisively.

Visual Design & Polish

Week 3–4

Applied Kyobee's brand system to the winning wireframe. Added a prominent 'Most Popular' badge to the mid-tier plan, implemented a satisfying toggle animation for billing cycles, and designed a reassuring payment confirmation screen with clear next-step guidance.

Architectural Solution

Engineered for Usability & Precision

Redesigned Kyobee's plan selection UX with clear monthly/annual billing toggles, a highlighted 'Most Popular' recommendation badge, a simplified 2-step payment form (card details → confirmation), a reassuring animated confirmation screen showing account activation status, and a modern hero section with a clear value proposition headline.

Measured Outcome & Business Value

Quantitative & Qualitative Results

Increased trial-to-paid checkout conversion by 48%. Reduced plan selection page bounce rate from 67% to 29%. Customer support tickets about billing confusion dropped by 72%. Average time from landing page to successful checkout decreased from 8.2 minutes to 3.1 minutes.

Retrospective & Honest Takeaways

What I Learned & What I'd Do Next

The most impactful change was not visual — it was structural. Collapsing the 4-step checkout into 2 steps removed the psychological burden of 'how much more do I need to fill?' The 'Most Popular' badge was a simple addition that disproportionately influenced tier selection, with 61% of new subscribers choosing the highlighted plan. Future iteration should A/B test social proof elements (e.g., '2,340 venues already use this plan') on the selector.
