Project Context & Background

Why This Project Existed

MSfitCentre is a fitness brand operating multiple gym locations. Their vision was 'Gym 2.0' — a smart gym experience where workout equipment has embedded sensors that automatically track sets, reps, and weight. The challenge was designing a companion mobile app that seamlessly bridges the physical gym experience with digital progress tracking, plus a marketing landing page to attract new memberships.

The Problem Statement

Friction & Roadblocks Faced by Users

Existing workout tracking apps (Strong, JEFIT, FitNotes) require manual entry of every exercise, set, rep count, and weight after each set. During intense workouts, users found this process disruptive — fumbling with phones between sets, losing focus, and often abandoning logging halfway through the session. The result: incomplete workout data, broken streak motivation, and eventual app abandonment. MSfitCentre needed an app that made logging feel invisible.

Research & Discovery

How We Listened to the User

▸Contextual Inquiry (Gym Floor): Spent 3 sessions (6 hours total) observing 12 gym members during their workouts. Documented phone usage patterns: when they check their phones, how they hold them (sweaty hands, one-handed), lighting conditions (harsh overhead fluorescents), and interruption frequency.
▸Competitive UX Teardown: Analyzed 5 leading workout apps (Strong, JEFIT, Hevy, FitNotes, Fitbod). Mapped their logging flows — average taps per set ranged from 4–7. Identified that all required the user to initiate and confirm each data point.
▸User Interviews: Conducted semi-structured interviews with 8 regular gym-goers. Key insight: 'I don't want to think about the app during my workout. I just want to lift and see my progress later.' Another: 'Rest timer is the only thing I actually use my phone for between sets.'
▸Ergonomic Analysis: Mapped thumb reach zones on 6.1" and 6.7" devices to ensure all primary actions fall within the natural one-handed comfort zone. Determined minimum tap target sizes for sweaty-finger conditions: 52×52px (vs standard 44×44px).
Step-by-Step Architecture

The Design & Iteration Process

Empathy Mapping

Week 1

Built empathy maps for 3 user archetypes: The Beginner (needs guidance, intimidated by gym culture), The Consistent (wants streak tracking and progressive overload data), and The Advanced (wants detailed analytics, volume tracking, PR alerts).

Information Architecture

Week 1–2

Designed the core app navigation: Today's Workout (auto-populated from sensor data), History (calendar + list view), Progress (muscle group heatmaps + strength charts), and Profile (streak counter, settings). Used card sorting with 8 participants to validate navigation labels.

Low-Fi Wireframes

Week 2–3

Sketched 24 screen wireframes on paper, then digitized in Figma. Focused on information density — showing just enough data per screen without scroll fatigue. The workout logging screen needed to show: current exercise name, set number, weight, reps, and rest timer, all within the top 60% of the viewport (thumb-reachable zone).

Hi-Fi Design & Motion

Week 3–5

Applied a dark theme optimized for gym lighting conditions (high contrast, OLED-friendly true blacks). Designed 1-tap set completion with haptic confirmation concept. Built auto-advancing rest countdown timers with progressive circle animations. Created muscle group recovery heatmaps using a traffic-light color system (green → recovered, yellow → moderate, red → needs rest).

Usability Testing

Week 5–6

Conducted moderated usability tests with 6 participants at the gym. Each participant completed a full simulated workout flow (3 exercises, 4 sets each). Measured: task completion rate (88%), time-on-task per set log (2.1 seconds average), error rate (0 critical errors), and subjective satisfaction (SUS score: 84/100).

Architectural Solution

Engineered for Usability & Precision

Designed Gym 2.0: an intuitive mobile app featuring 1-tap set completion with haptic feedback, automatic rest countdowns that start when a set is logged, visual muscle group recovery heatmaps, progressive overload tracking with PR celebration animations, and a high-converting companion web landing page with membership CTA optimization.

Measured Outcome & Business Value

Quantitative & Qualitative Results

Achieved an 88% workout session completion rate in usability testing (vs. industry average of ~40% for manual logging apps). Zero critical errors during testing. SUS usability score of 84/100 (classified as 'Excellent'). The landing page wireframe received enthusiastic approval from MSfitCentre's marketing team for immediate development.

Retrospective & Honest Takeaways

What I Learned & What I'd Do Next

The key insight was that the best workout app is one you barely notice using. By anchoring the design around auto-syncing sensor data and minimizing required user input, we flipped the paradigm from 'logging workouts' to 'workouts logging themselves.' The dark theme wasn't just aesthetic — under harsh gym fluorescents, light themes cause screen glare that makes the app unusable. If I were to iterate, I'd add a social layer for workout buddies and integrate Apple Watch complications for even less phone interaction.
